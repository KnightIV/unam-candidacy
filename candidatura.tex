\section*{Abstract}
The study of stellar clusters (SCs) within the Galaxy serves an important role in understanding the Galaxy’s physical and chemical characteristics. SCs are defined as gravitationally-bound groups of stars born from a single, homogeneous molecular cloud, typically composed of a single stellar population. This allows us to measure physical parameters such as a cluster’s age, metallicity, distance, and extinction, which can be determined through the photometric data of resolved cluster members. Studies of Galactic clusters have seen a significant increase in recent years with the advent of Gaia, whose unmatched astrometric precision has allowed for the identification of thousands of new stellar clusters, as well as refining membership lists for previously identified clusters.

This doctoral thesis project makes use of Gaia DR3 (and DR4 in the near future) data to update and expand the membership lists from the Hunt \& Reffert (2024) and the Unified Cluster Catalog (UCC). By using HDBSCAN we identify an additional population of stars for each cluster, retrieving stars beyond the main clusters’ cores and at fainter magnitudes than previously identified. We determine each cluster’s physical parameters using isochrone fitting methods using \textit{ASteCA} and \code{simul\_fit} on each clusters' Gaia DR3 data. We compare the derived fundamental cluster parameters between both methods as well as the reported literature values. With these preliminary results we have validated the efficacy of the membership determination pipeline and cluster parameter inference for future use with the rest of the cataloged observations made from the Observatorio Astronómico Universitario -- San Pedro Mártir (SPM).
