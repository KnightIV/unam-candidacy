\section{Conclusions and Future Work}

In this work we have analyzed four well-studied OCs as a first test of the membership determination pipeline as well as photometric analysis tooling employed in this doctoral thesis project. As shown in Table \ref{tab:param-comparison}, our inferred parameters are in general agreement with those recorded in the literature for all clusters; the exception to this are the values of $E(B - V)$ derived with ASteCA. The main reason for this discrepancy is the impact of unresolved binary stars on the Gaia CMDs, as can be seen in Figure \ref{fig:binarity-probability}. Using the fitted parameters $\alpha$ and $\beta$, we obtain the following binary fractions for each cluster: 0.77 (NGC 1245), 0.82 (NGC 2311), 0.88 (NGC 2420), and 0.52 (NGC 2506). These are in general much higher than the binary fractions reported in the UCC: 0.36 (NGC 1245), 0.47 (NGC 2311), 0.455 (NGC 2420), and 0.2 (NGC 2506). While this may be caused by a possible degeneracy between a cluster's $E(B - V)$ and binarity parameters, a more in depth analysis still needs to be performed. This includes using sampling techniques such as \textit{Markov Chain Monte-Carlo} (MCMC) instead or in conjunction with the ABC methodology employed in this study, which can give a better look into the posterior probability distribution function for the cluster fundamental parameters \autocite{craiuApproximateMethodsBayesian2023}.

While this work uses the well-established HDBSCAN method to identify new member stars for each cluster, it is also possible to retrieve former-member stars that have been ejected so far that they are no longer gravitationally bound. By searching in regions of interest (derived from simulating three-body ejections over a small timescale) for stars of similar chemical composition it is possible to identify extra-tidal stars that were recently ejected \autocite{grondinSearchingExtratidalStars2023}. This can be further explored for the more populated clusters in the SPM catalog of observations.
